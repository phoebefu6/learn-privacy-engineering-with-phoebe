The release environment

Part 0

Where we start

Leader session 1 ended with a number: on six ordinary columns, 17.18% of Brightwater's real rows are alone. That number describes the data. PDPC's test has a second limb, and it is the one most release reviews skip: what the recipient has or is likely to have access to, and the measures and safeguards around the file. Tonight Dr Hana Seow, Brightwater's research director, is weighing three ways to get the extract to her three university partners. Lim Su-Ann, the DPO, has to say which ones are safe, at what setting, and under which controls. Brightwater and its people are constructed; the 32,561 rows from the UCI Adult extract of the 1994 US Census are real.

Part 1 · covers the second limb: risk = data + environment

The same file, three different risks

5 min live

PDPC's guidelines (Selected Topics, para 3.4) weigh two things together: the data, alone or combined with other information the recipient has or is likely to have access to, and the measures and safeguards, or the lack of them. Para 3.6 lists the factors: the amount of alteration the data has been subjected to, the extent of disclosure, the availability of other relevant information, and the recipients' ability and motivation to re-identify. Only the first factor lives in the file. The other three are set by the route you choose.

LiveWhat each route changes3 min▶

Public open data. Extent of disclosure is total and permanent: once posted, it cannot be recalled. The other information an outsider can bring is everything public, including releases nobody has made yet. Ability and motivation are unknown, so you must assume the worst reasonable case. Only the data limb is left for you to work with, and it has to carry the whole load.

Named partners under an agreement. Disclosure is limited to people you can list. What they can bring is what a researcher on those teams plausibly knows. Their motivation to re-identify is lowered by the agreement they signed: PDPC's para 3.34 notes motivation may be low where there are legal barriers such as contractual obligations. Both limbs now share the load.

An enclave. Researchers come to the data, run queries inside a controlled environment, and only results leave. Nobody outside ever holds a row, so the file's risk moves to its outputs: every table or model that leaves has to be checked for small counts and single people before it goes. The environment does most of the work.

Self-studyAnonymised is a state you keep, not a stamp2 min▶

Para 3.5 of the guidelines says anonymised data is no longer considered personal data, and in the same breath that the effectiveness of anonymisation may degrade over time, so it should be reviewed periodically. Para 3.6 adds that it must be reassessed when the data is combined with other information. The environment is what degrades: a new public dataset appears, a partner team grows, a copy turns up somewhere it should not be. A route that was safe at sign-off needs a review date, and Leader session 4 writes that date into the sign-off memo.

Whether a given file is personal data in law is a legal question; it belongs to PDPA and DNC ↗. This session measures the risk and designs the environment.

Real world

The Netflix Prize was a public route. Netflix released movie ratings from about 500,000 subscribers with names replaced by numbers, open to anyone entering the competition. Narayanan and Shmatikov brought one thing the release never considered, public IMDb reviews, and identified the records of known users. Nothing in the file changed between the release and the attack. The environment had already decided the outcome: a public route lets any outsider bring any public source.

Part 2 · covers recipient controls

Controls that bind the recipient

5 min live

Once you know who receives the file, you can build walls around them. PDPC's para 3.7(c) lists them: limit the number of recipients, restrict use and subsequent disclosure, require recipients to run processes for proper use, and destroy the data when it is no longer needed. Para 3.23 adds contractual safeguards or an undertaking not to re-identify. Each wall stops a different thing going wrong, and none of them makes the rows themselves any safer.

LiveThe walls, in the guideline's own order3 min▶

Limit and name the recipients (para 3.7(c)). "Three university teams" is not a list. A list has names, and a rule for adding one. Every extra reader is one more person who might know something about someone in the file.

Restrict use and subsequent disclosure (para 3.7(c)). The wall that stops the file travelling: the extract may be used only for the stated study and may not be passed to anyone outside the list. Without it, a careful release to three teams becomes an uncontrolled release to whoever they trust.

Proper-use processes and destruction (para 3.7(c)). Recipients must run processes that keep the data used as agreed, and destroy it when it is no longer needed. A copy that outlives the project is a copy nobody is watching.

No re-identification (paras 3.18 and 3.23). Para 3.18 says organisations will still need contractual safeguards against re-identification even where the recipient sits outside the PDPA. Para 3.23 names the form: contractual safeguards, or an undertaking not to re-identify. Para 3.42 gives contract examples worth reading before you draft your own.

Self-studyWhat a public route can still bind2 min▶

Open data has no recipient list, so most of the walls fall away. Para 3.12 asks organisations releasing open data to consider legal safeguards, for example prohibiting unauthorised intentional re-identification in the terms of use. A licence term is the only wall left, and nobody signs it in front of you. That is why a public route leans almost entirely on the data limb.

The guidelines also state that the unauthorised re-identification of anonymised information is an offence under Part 9B of the PDPA (para 3.18). Treat that as the guideline's statement; what the Act provides, and when it applies, is covered in PDPA and DNC ↗. An offence deters a careful researcher; it does not deter someone who never expects to be traced.

For a published count table, the small-cell rule that keeps single people out of the cells is taught in Smart City ↗. For who may query what inside your own walls, see Data Access Control ↗.

Real world

Brightwater's draft clause 7. Lim Su-Ann's first draft of the partner agreement reads: "The Recipient shall use the Extract only for the Approved Study; shall not disclose it, or any part of it, to any person not named in Schedule A; shall not attempt to re-identify any individual, or link the Extract with any other information for that purpose; and shall destroy all copies within 30 days of the end of the Approved Study and confirm destruction in writing." Four walls in one clause, each matched to a paragraph of the guidelines. Constructed Brightwater example; the 30 days is her choice, not a standard.

Part 3 · covers the realistic intruder

Who is the realistic intruder?

5 min live

Walls are tested by someone trying to get past them. PDPC calls running a motivated intruder test good practice (para 3.11), and paras 3.36 to 3.38 describe the intruder, adapted from the UK ICO's code: motivated, reasonably competent, with the internet and public directories, not targeting any particular individual, and with no criminal methods or specialist equipment. Para 3.38(a) asks you to count the technical, legal, regulatory and organisational controls in place. The question for each route is which intruders it must withstand on the data alone.

LiveSetting k for the route3 min▶

PDPC's numbers. Para 3.9 sets a minimum k-anonymity value of 5 for sharing with external parties, and a lower minimum of 3 for internal sharing with controls. Other methods are acceptable if they show a similar or lower risk of re-identification. k counts how many people share each combination of the columns an outsider could know; k = 5 means nobody is in a group smaller than five.

If 5 is out of reach. Para 3.10 says that where k = 5 cannot be reached, more stringent safeguards are needed, for example tracking data lineage and disposing of the data after use. The environment is asked to make up for what the data cannot carry.

The number is a floor, and contested above it. 5 is a minimum for external sharing, not a target for open data. How high a public release should go is not settled in the guidance or the literature, and Engineer session 3 shows why k on its own can still leak. Say which k you set and why, rather than borrowing a number.

Self-studyThe ICO's version, and its currency2 min▶

PDPC's test is adapted from the UK ICO. The ICO's anonymisation guidance, published 28 March 2025, asks you to consider all practical steps reasonably likely to be used by someone motivated to identify people, and says you should adopt a motivated intruder test as part of your risk assessment. The archived copy read for these pages carries a banner that the guidance is under review, because the Data (Use and Access) Act came into law on 19 June 2025. Whether it has since been revised could not be confirmed; check the live ICO page before quoting it. For UK and EU law, see GDPR ↗.

Real world

AOL's intruders were reporters. In 2006 AOL posted about 20 million web search queries with user names replaced by numbers: a public route. Reporters from The New York Times read the searches of user 4417749, followed the towns, shops and a surname in them, and found Thelma Arnold, a 62-year-old widow in Lilburn, Georgia. No hacking, no special tools, no criminal methods: exactly the motivated, reasonably competent intruder PDPC's test describes. A public route has to assume that person will look.

Step 5 · Measure what is left. The desk below starts at rung 4: five columns coarsened, every class under 5 suppressed. Read the single-row matches and the expected re-identifications among the 1,000 people an outsider knows. Then click rung 6 and read them again. The widget prints 0 single matches and 16.7 expected at rung 4 (725 rows suppressed, 2.23%), and 0 single matches and 4.4 expected at rung 6 (k 10, l 3, 345 rows suppressed, 1.06%). ★ Step 5 · the register linerung k single expected suppressed could support 4 5 ___ _____ _____ ______________ 6 10 ___ _____ _____ ______________ Brightwater: rung 4 (0, 16.7) for partners with all four walls; rung 6 (0, 4.4) the least for public, plus an intruder test; enclave: either, with every output checked before it leaves.

"0 single matches" is not "0 risk". At rung 4 nobody is alone, yet the expected count is still 16.7 of 1,000, because people in a class of five can each be guessed one time in five. That residue is why the route matters: on the partner route four walls stand around it, on the public route nothing does. Engineer session 4 builds the attack behind these numbers.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 2 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · PDPC's serious-possibility test weighs two limbs. Which two?

Para 3.4 puts the data and the environment in the same test. The file is only half of it; who receives it, what they hold and what binds them is the other half.

2 · A partner team wants to give a copy to a fourth university. Which control is meant to stop that?

Para 3.7(c) lists restricting use and subsequent disclosure as a recipient control. Changing k changes the rows, not where they travel, and an offence deters re-identification rather than sharing.

3 · Why can the same Brightwater file be acceptable for the named-partner route and not for open data?

The rows are identical on both routes. The extent of disclosure, the other information available and the recipients' motivation all change, so the data limb has to carry more on the public route.
