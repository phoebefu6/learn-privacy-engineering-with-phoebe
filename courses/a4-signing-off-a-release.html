Part 1 · covers thresholds you can defend

A threshold is a judgement with a reason

5 min live

PDPC gives you a floor, not a finish line. Its guidelines set a minimum k of 5 for sharing with external parties and a lower minimum of 3 for internal sharing with controls, and they allow other methods that show a similar or lower risk of re-identification. A sign-off weighs what was measured against what the data is still good for, and both sit on the route's controls. Change the controls and the same numbers weigh differently.

LiveWhat PDPC actually sets, and what it leaves to you3 min▶

A floor by audience. PDPC's Advisory Guidelines on Selected Topics (para 3.9) set a minimum k of 5 for sharing with external parties and 3 for internal sharing, with controls. The Guide to Basic Anonymisation (2022) says the industry threshold is generally 3 or 5, a higher value such as 5 or more for external sharing and a lower one such as 3 for internal sharing or long-term retention (p24 to 25).

An escape hatch with a price. Para 3.9 accepts other methods if they show a similar or lower risk of re-identification. Para 3.10 says that where k of 5 cannot be reached, the organisation should put more stringent safeguards in place, for example tracking the data's lineage and disposing of it after use. Missing the number moves the weight onto the controls; it never lowers the bar quietly.

A teaching bar is not a standard. The desk you used in Leader session 2 marks k of at least 10, l of at least 3 and under 5% of rows suppressed as its bar. It is a rule of thumb for practice. Researchers and regulators disagree on the right values, and no single number is endorsed everywhere. On a sign-off, write the number and the reason next to it, so the next reader can disagree with the reason rather than guess at it.

Self-studySigning an epsilon: the number has a unit3 min▶

When the route is noisy counts rather than rows, the number on the sign-off is epsilon. NIST's Guidelines for Evaluating Differential Privacy Guarantees (SP 800-226, March 2025) draws the components of a guarantee as a pyramid and says each is vital to a meaningful guarantee. Epsilon is only one of them; others include the unit of privacy, the trust model, the query model, security, access control and side channels.

The unit matters most for a leader. NIST's key takeaway is that user-level privacy gives stronger guarantees than attribute-level, user-day-level or event-level privacy, and that guarantees with different privacy units are not directly comparable. An epsilon of 1 per person and an epsilon of 1 per event are different promises. So the sign-off line reads "epsilon, per what, over how many queries", never epsilon alone. Engineer session 6 shows how a budget is spent across queries.

Real world

"The guide says 3 or 5, so 3." Ravi Menon's first draft set k to 3 to keep more detail for the partners. Lim Su-Ann sent it back with one line: the partners are three university teams outside Brightwater, so this is external sharing, and para 3.9 sets the minimum at 5; the 3 is for internal sharing with controls. Ravi re-ran the desk at k 10 instead, because the partners wanted more columns than the draft had and she wanted the margin in writing. Constructed Brightwater example; the paragraph numbers are real.

Part 2 · covers the attack test

Run the attack on the list that ships

5 min live

A threshold says how big the classes are. An attack test says what an outsider can actually do. PDPC calls running a motivated intruder test good practice (para 3.11), and its Guide puts the measurement in Step 4, compute your risk, before Step 5, manage your re-identification and disclosure risks. The test is only as good as the columns it was run on. On the real rows, rung 4 passes with k 5 and 0 single matches; ship workclass alongside it without declaring it, and the same attack finds 22 people.

LiveWhat the attack test has to prove3 min▶

Who the intruder is. PDPC's paras 3.36 to 3.38 describe the motivated intruder, adapted from the UK ICO's code: motivated and reasonably competent, using the internet and public directories, not targeting a particular person, with no criminal methods or specialist equipment. Para 3.38(a) asks you to count the technical, legal, regulatory and organisational controls in place.

What the intruder holds. On the desk, an outsider knows six facts exactly about 1,000 real people in the file and joins on them. Single matches are people found in a class of one; expected re-identifications add up one guess per person over their class size. Both go on the sign-off.

Which columns. Every column in the file that leaves the building, declared or not. A k computed on the reviewed list was never wrong, only incomplete: after the break the desk still prints k 5, while single matches go from 0 to 22. The fix is a rule of procedure: the attack is re-run on the final column list, and the signature is dated after that run.

Self-studySteps 4 and 5 in PDPC's Guide2 min▶

The Guide to Basic Anonymisation (2022) runs five steps: know your data, de-identify it, apply anonymisation techniques, compute your risk, and manage your re-identification and disclosure risks. Step 4 is where the measured number comes from, k and its equivalents. Step 5 names three kinds of disclosure to manage: identity disclosure (finding the person), attribute disclosure (learning something about them, as the 89 revealed occupations do) and inference disclosure. A sign-off that reports only k has done Step 4 for identity and skipped the rest. The Guide is written for structured, non-complex data and suggests bringing in experts where appropriate.

Real world

Netflix shipped what the attackers needed. In 2006 Netflix published movie ratings from about 500,000 subscribers for a public prediction prize, with names replaced by numbers, so someone inside had judged the file safe to publish. Narayanan and Shmatikov showed that 8 ratings, 2 of which could be wrong, with dates known to within 14 days, single out 99% of the records, and with public IMDb reviews as background knowledge they identified the records of known users. The ratings were the quasi-identifiers. A review that looked at the names and not at the ratings signed the wrong list.

Part 3 · covers the release record and review

A record someone can check, and a date to check it

5 min live

PDPC's para 3.5 says the effectiveness of anonymisation may degrade over time and should be reviewed periodically, and para 3.6 says it must be reassessed when the data is combined with other information. A signature is therefore a claim about a moment. The release record makes the moment checkable: every column and whether it was declared, the setting, the results, the break test, the route and the review date. Some triggers you schedule; others arrive by email.

LiveWhat the record holds, and why each field is there3 min▶

Columns, each marked declared yes or no. The break in Part 2 lives here. If a column ships and is not on the declared list, the record shows it before an outsider does.

Setting and results. How coarse each column went out, the k, and what the desk measured: k, l, t, rows suppressed, precision, single matches and expected re-identifications. A second engineer with the same rows and the record must get the same numbers; that is the test of the record.

Break test. The attack re-run with any column a partner might add, and the decision taken: generalise and declare it, or leave it out.

Sign-off. Who signed, the route and its controls, and the review date. Engineer session 8 builds these fields; tonight you read them as the person whose name goes at the bottom.

Self-studyA published budget as a sign-off number2 min▶

A sign-off number can be public. The US Census Bureau's press release CB21-CN.42 (9 June 2021) announced "a total privacy-loss budget for the redistricting data product" of epsilon 19.61, which includes 17.14 for the persons file and 2.47 for the housing unit data (17.14 + 2.47 = 19.61). The release names the TopDown Algorithm and the privacy-loss budget; the Bureau's adoption of differential privacy for 2020 is widely reported. The figure covers the redistricting product only, not the whole of the 2020 Census output, and a budget for later files is not quoted here because no primary source for one was read.

What a leader takes from it: the number was written down, split by file, and published before release, so anyone can argue with it. That is the standard a privately signed epsilon should meet too, with its unit and its query count beside it.

Real world

Lim Su-Ann's signature block. Brightwater's memo ends in four lines she will not sign without: "Signed on the final column list, re-run on the date below. Route: three named partner teams under the data-sharing agreement, clause 7. Does not protect against: any column added after this date, any new outside data, a partner who breaks clause 7. Review: twelve months from release, or at the first trigger, whichever is sooner." Constructed Brightwater example; the twelve months is her choice, not a standard.

Build-along

Sign Brightwater's release

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Ravi Menon has run the desk and brought the release record for the partner extract at rung 6. Dr Hana Seow wants the file with the three university teams this month. Lim Su-Ann will sign only a one-page memo she could hand to a regulator or a successor. Read the numbers from the desk, press the break, name the route, set the review, and write the memo, including the sentence most memos leave out.

Step 1 · Read rung 6 from the desk. The desk below opens on rung 6: age in 20-year bands, education as degree yes or no, race, marital status and country grouped, every class under 10 suppressed. Read the six numbers a signer needs. The widget prints k 10, l 3, 345 rows suppressed (1.06%), precision 51%, 0 single matches and 4.4 expected re-identifications among the 1,000 people the outsider knows, and its verdict says the desk's bar is passed. Write them in field 4.

★ Step 1 · field 4, resultsk ___ l ___ suppressed ____% precision ___% single matches ___ of 1,000 expected re-identifications ____ Brightwater, rung 6: k 10 · l 3 · 1.06% (345 rows) · 51% 0 single · 4.4 expected

Step 2 · Press the break button. On the same desk, press "Break it: publish workclass too". It sends workclass out exact without declaring it, which is what would happen if a partner asked for it after review. k still reads 10, because k only counts the declared columns. The widget prints 3 single matches, 25.2 expected re-identifications and 64 occupations revealed, and the verdict turns to not releasable. Rung 6 survives better than rung 4 did (22 single matches, 73.5 expected, 89 revealed) and still fails. Write it in field 5 with the decision.

★ Step 2 · field 5, break testwith ________ added, undeclared: single ___ expected ____ revealed ___ decision: generalise and declare it / leave it out Brightwater: workclass -> 3 single · 25.2 expected · 64 revealed decision: workclass is not published in this release

Step 3 · Name the route and its controls. Take the route and the walls from your Leader session 2 risk register. Brightwater's partner route is three named university teams under one agreement, with clause 7 carrying the four walls: use only for the approved study, no disclosure beyond the named people, no attempt to re-identify or link, destroy within 30 days of the end. Rung 6 is stricter than PDPC's external minimum of 5; say why in one line.

★ Step 3 · field 1, route and controlsroute: ______________________ people who see rows: ____ walls: limit recipients Y/N · no onward disclosure Y/N no re-identification Y/N · destroy by ________ k chosen: ___ because ______________________________________ Brightwater: 3 partner teams, about 20 people, clause 7, all four walls k 10, above PDPC's minimum of 5, for margin against added columns

Step 4 · Set the review triggers and the date. List what would make you re-run the desk before the date comes: a partner asking for a column, a new public dataset that overlaps the six facts, a partner team growing, a copy turning up outside the named list. Then set the date itself. Para 3.5 asks for periodic review without fixing a period, so the period is yours to choose and defend.

★ Step 4 · field 6, reviewre-run if: ________________________________________________ ________________________________________________ review date: __________ owner: __________________ Brightwater: any column request · new outside data on the six facts · team changes · a copy outside Schedule A. Review: 12 months, Lim Su-Ann

Step 5 · Write the memo, and the sentence on what it does not protect against. Put the six fields on one page, sign under them, and write one plain sentence naming what the release leaves open. Rung 6 does not make the residual zero: 4.4 of 1,000 known people can still be guessed by chance, and the guarantee holds only for the columns and the route on the page.

★ Step 5 · the memoRelease: Brightwater partner extract, rung 6, final column list 1 route 3 partner teams under agreement, clause 7 2 columns six declared; workclass not published 3 setting age 20y, degree yes/no, race/marital/country grouped, k 10 4 results k 10 · l 3 · 1.06% · 51% · 0 single · 4.4 expected 5 break workclass undeclared: 3 single · 25.2 · 64 revealed 6 limits This release does not protect against: a column added after today, new outside data on the six facts, a partner who breaks clause 7, or guessing at the 4.4 expected level. It is not approved for any public route. Signed: Lim Su-Ann, DPO · review: 12 months or first trigger

The sixth field is the one that ages well. Numbers go stale the day a new dataset appears; a clear sentence about what was never promised does not. When someone asks in a year why a partner was allowed this file, the limits line and the review date are what show the decision was made with its eyes open.

Homework

Before your next release

30 min
  1. Schedule the re-review. For the release you have followed since Leader session 1, put a review date in the diary of a named owner today, and list the triggers that would bring it forward.
  2. Check the column list. Compare the columns that were reviewed with the columns that actually shipped. If any column shipped undeclared, ask for the attack to be re-run on the final list.
  3. Read the source. Read paras 3.5, 3.6, 3.9 to 3.11 and 3.36 to 3.38 of PDPC's Advisory Guidelines on Selected Topics, Chapter 3, and Steps 4 and 5 of the Guide to Basic Anonymisation. Underline what para 3.10 asks for when k of 5 cannot be reached.
  4. Write the sixth field. Draft the one sentence on what that release does not protect against, and send it to whoever signed it. If nobody signed it, that is the finding.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 4 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Brightwater is sharing its extract with three university teams outside the institute. What minimum k do PDPC's guidelines set for this kind of sharing?

Para 3.9 sets 5 for external parties and 3 for internal sharing with controls. The desk's k of 10 is a teaching bar, not a standard, and para 3.10 asks for stricter safeguards, not a lower bar, when 5 cannot be reached.

2 · At rung 4 the desk prints k 5 and 0 single matches. Workclass then ships undeclared and single matches jump to 22, while k still reads 5. Why does the sign-off need the final column list?

The k was never wrong, only incomplete. The attack test has to run on what leaves the building, and the record marks every column declared or not so the gap shows before an outsider finds it.

3 · When should a signed release be reviewed again?

Para 3.5 says effectiveness may degrade over time and asks for periodic review; para 3.6 asks for reassessment when the data is combined with other information. The date is yours to set; the triggers arrive on their own.
