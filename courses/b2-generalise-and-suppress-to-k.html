Part 1 · covers k-anonymity

k is the size of the smallest crowd

5 min live

Latanya Sweeney gave the measure its name in 2002. A release satisfies k-anonymity when every combination of quasi-identifier values in it appears at least k times. Put plainly: anyone an outsider looks up by those values lands in a group of at least k people, and "cannot be distinguished from at least k-1 individuals" in the release. k is never a property of a column. It is the size of the smallest equivalence class, which is why one lone row anywhere in the file sets k to 1.

LiveWhat k counts, and what it does not3 min▶

k counts quasi-identifiers only. Singapore's Personal Data Protection Commission says the same in its Guide to Basic Anonymisation (2022): direct identifiers are removed first, and k is computed over the indirect identifiers. Leave a name in and k is 1 whatever you do to age.

k depends on which columns you declared. k of 5 on six columns says nothing about a seventh column you also published. Engineer session 4 shows how one undeclared column breaks a k you are proud of.

k says nothing about the sensitive column. Five people who share their quasi-identifiers and all have the same occupation are 5-anonymous, and an outsider still learns the occupation. That gap is Engineer session 3.

Self-studyWhich k? What the guidance says, and what it leaves open3 min▶

PDPC's guide (p24) states that "the industry threshold for k-anonymity value is at 3 or 5", and asks for a higher value, 5 or more, for sharing data outside the organisation, with a lower value such as 3 acceptable for internal sharing or long-term retention. It does not claim that 5 is safe in every setting. The research literature has never agreed on one number either: the right k depends on who receives the file, what they could join it with, and what controls bind them. Treat 3 and 5 as the floor a reviewer will expect, and argue upward from the release environment. What the PDPA requires of an anonymised release is a legal question; PDPA and DNC ↗ owns it.

Real world

The regulator's own worked example. When PDPC walks through computing re-identification risk in its Guide to Basic Anonymisation, the example dataset is taxi data and the k it works to is 5. That is the threshold Brightwater's reviewers will look for first.

Part 2 · covers generalisation hierarchies

Climb a column one step at a time

5 min live

Generalisation replaces a value with a broader one: an exact age with a band, a specific degree with a level. PDPC lists it among the basic techniques in Annex A of its guide. The engineering discipline is to write the steps down before you start, as a hierarchy for each quasi-identifier, from exact at the bottom to removed at the top. Then every setting is a choice of one level per column, it can be reproduced, and the release record can name it.

LiveThe six hierarchies the desk uses4 min▶

Age: exact, 5-year bands, 10-year bands, 20-year bands (17-36, 37-56 and so on, starting at the youngest age in the file), removed.

Education: the 16 exact values, 5 levels (No high school diploma, High school, Some college or associate, Bachelors, Postgraduate), degree yes or no, removed.

Race: exact, White or not white, removed. Marital status: exact, three groups (Married, Never married, Previously married), removed. Country of birth: the 42 exact values, three groups (United States, Outside US, Unknown), removed. Sex: exact or removed.

Rung 3 takes age, race, marital status, education and country one step each and keeps sex exact. Uniqueness falls from 17.18% to 0.57%, and classes from 8,553 to 872.

Self-studyDesigning a hierarchy that analysts can live with3 min▶

Each step must merge, never split. Every value at one level belongs to exactly one value at the next. 5-year bands nest inside 10-year bands; a 7-year band would not, and two settings could then no longer be compared.

Follow the analysis, not the alphabet. Education's 5 levels keep the boundaries labour economists care about (a diploma, a degree, a postgraduate qualification). Grouping "Some college" with "Bachelors" would be the same size and far less useful.

Give rare values somewhere to go. Country of birth has 42 values, and most of them hold a handful of people. "Outside US" is a crude group, and it is the step that takes most migrants out of classes of one. A missing value ("?") deserves its own group, "Unknown", rather than being folded silently into another.

Write it down as data. A hierarchy kept as a mapping table in the repository can be reviewed, versioned and reused at the next release. A hierarchy kept in someone's head is a different release every time.

Constructed Brightwater example

Ravi bands age and stops. Ravi's first instinct is the obvious column: he releases age in 10-year bands and keeps the rest exact. The desk prints 7.18% of rows still alone, rung 2, down from 17.18% but nowhere near safe, because education and country of birth are still doing the identifying. Only when five columns each climb one step does uniqueness drop to 0.57%. One coarse column rarely fixes a file; the identifying is shared out across the combination.

Part 3 · covers suppression and what k costs

Suppression removes the people who are hardest to hide

5 min live

Generalisation alone leaves some classes small: at rung 3, 0.57% of rows are still alone and k is 1. Record suppression, also in PDPC's Annex A, removes those rows from the release. Set k, drop every class smaller than k, and what remains satisfies k-anonymity by construction. It is cheap to code and expensive in a way that does not show up in a k value: the rows you drop are not a random sample.

LiveTwo ways to reach k, two different bills4 min▶

Suppress more. Keep rung 3's generalisation and raise k. k of 5 removes 725 rows (2.23%); k of 10 removes 1,801 (5.53%). Precision stays at 61% because no column got coarser. The bill is paid in people.

Generalise more. Rung 6 bands age in 20 years and reduces education to degree yes or no, then asks for k of 10. Classes fall from 872 to 227, so far fewer of them are small, and only 345 rows (1.06%) are suppressed. Precision falls to 51%. The bill is paid in detail.

Read precision for what it is. The desk's precision is a rule of thumb, one minus the average height climbed on the hierarchies: 100% at rung 1, 92% at rung 2, 61% at rungs 3 to 5, 51% at rung 6. It is useful for comparing settings on the same file and says nothing about whether a particular analysis still works. Ask the partner which relationship they need, and test that.

Self-studyWho gets suppressed3 min▶

Small classes are made of rare combinations, so suppression removes the rare first. Computed on the real rows at rung 4 (k of 5): 10.4% of the whole file was born outside the US or has an unknown country of birth, but 68.1% of the 725 suppressed rows were. 14.6% of the file is not white; 46.8% of the suppressed rows are not white. Ravi's release would be 5-anonymous and would quietly under-represent migrants and minorities.

That matters twice. A partner studying migrant earnings loses the people the study is about. And a release that silently drops the hard cases looks better on k than its environment deserves. Record the count, record who it fell on, and tell the recipient. Where suppression would remove a group the analysis depends on, generalise that column further instead, or move the release to a more controlled route; Leader session 2 covers the routes.

Constructed Brightwater example

Hana's partner wants 5-year age bands. One of the three university teams is fitting an earnings regression and says 10-year bands blur the age curve. Ravi tries it on the desk: age in 5-year bands, the other four columns as at rung 3, k of 5. Uniqueness before suppression rises from 0.57% to 1.07%, and suppression rises from 725 rows to 1,375 (4.22%). Precision goes up from 61% to 65%. Hana now has a real trade to put to the partner: finer ages for everyone, or 650 more real people missing from the file, most of them the rare ones.

★ Step 4 · pandas, generalise and suppress to kimport pandas as pd cols = ["age", "workclass", "fnlwgt", "education", "education_num", "marital", "occupation", "relationship", "race", "sex", "capital_gain", "capital_loss", "hours", "country", "income"] df = pd.read_csv("adult.data", names=cols, skipinitialspace=True) no_diploma = ["Preschool", "1st-4th", "5th-6th", "7th-8th", "9th", "10th", "11th", "12th"] some_college = ["Some-college", "Assoc-voc", "Assoc-acdm"] postgrad = ["Masters", "Prof-school", "Doctorate"] edu5 = {**{e: "No high school diploma" for e in no_diploma}, "HS-grad": "High school", **{e: "Some college or associate" for e in some_college}, "Bachelors": "Bachelors", **{e: "Postgraduate" for e in postgrad}} married = ["Married-civ-spouse", "Married-spouse-absent", "Married-AF-spouse"] g = pd.DataFrame({ "age": (df.age // 10 * 10).astype(int), "sex": df.sex, "race": df.race.where(df.race == "White", "Not white"), "marital": df.marital.map(lambda m: "Married" if m in married else "Never married" if m == "Never-married" else "Previously married"), "education": df.education.map(edu5), "country": df.country.map(lambda c: "United States" if c == "United-States" else "Unknown" if c == "?" else "Outside US"), }) k = 5 qi = list(g.columns) size = g.groupby(qi)["age"].transform("size") released = g[size >= k] print("classes:", g.groupby(qi).ngroups) print("rows suppressed:", int((size < k).sum()), f"({100 * (size < k).mean():.2f}%)") print("k achieved:", released.groupby(qi).size().min()) # classes: 872 # rows suppressed: 725 (2.23%) # k achieved: 5

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Engineer session 2 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Brightwater's release satisfies k-anonymity with k of 5 on six quasi-identifiers. What does that guarantee?

k is the size of the smallest class on the declared quasi-identifiers. It says nothing about undeclared columns or about the sensitive column.

2 · Rung 5 and rung 6 both ask for k of 10. Why does rung 6 suppress 345 rows while rung 5 suppresses 1,801?

More generalisation makes bigger classes, so fewer of them fall under k. The bill moves from people (rows suppressed) to detail (precision 61% down to 51%).

3 · Why is record suppression not a neutral step?

Suppression is targeted by construction. Record what it removed and who it fell on, and tell the recipient.
