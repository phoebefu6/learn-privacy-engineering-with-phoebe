Finding the quasi-identifiers

Brightwater's extract has no names, no ID numbers and no addresses. It still identifies people, because a handful of ordinary facts, age, sex, race, marital status, education and country of birth, are enough to make most of a group look different from everyone else. Part 1 · covers the three kinds of column

Every release has three kinds of column

5 min live

Before any technique, sort the columns. A direct identifier names a person on its own: name, NRIC or passport number, phone, email. A quasi-identifier is harmless alone and identifying in combination: age, sex, postcode, date of birth, job title. A sensitive column is the thing the release is about and the thing an attacker wants: income, diagnosis, occupation, a claim amount. Removing the first kind is the easy part, and it is where most releases stop.

LiveThe test for a quasi-identifier3 min▶

Could someone outside know it? A column is a quasi-identifier when a person's neighbour, colleague, social media profile or another public dataset could tell an outsider its value. Age, sex and marital status pass easily. Your internal risk score usually does not, because nobody outside has it.

Does it vary between people? A column where everyone has the same value identifies nobody. Country of birth is "United-States" for 29,170 of the 32,561 rows, so on its own it barely separates anyone; for the 3,391 rows not marked US-born (2,808 born elsewhere, 583 unknown) it separates a great deal.

Is it sensitive as well? Some columns are both. Occupation is something an attacker wants and also something a colleague knows. Treat a column that is both as a quasi-identifier for the attack and as sensitive for the harm.

Self-studyWhat PDPC calls them2 min▶

Singapore's Personal Data Protection Commission sorts the columns the same way in its Guide to Basic Anonymisation (2022): direct identifiers are removed or replaced first, and k is then counted over the indirect identifiers only. The words differ between guides and papers ("quasi-identifier" is the research term, "indirect identifier" the guidance term), the idea does not. Whether a dataset that passes all this is still personal data under the PDPA is a legal question; PDPA and DNC ↗ owns it.

Real world

The governor's medical record. In the 1990s the Massachusetts Group Insurance Commission released state employees' hospital records with names removed. Latanya Sweeney bought the Cambridge voter roll and joined it on ZIP code, date of birth and sex; the governor's record was the only one that matched. None of the three columns was a direct identifier. All three were quasi-identifiers, and the voter roll was the outsider's knowledge.

Part 2 · covers equivalence classes

An equivalence class is everyone who looks the same

5 min live

Group the rows by their quasi-identifier values. Each group is an equivalence class: inside it, an outsider who knows those values cannot tell the people apart. Its size is the number that matters. A class of one is a person the outsider can point at. Every measure in the next seven sessions, k, l, t, the linkage hit rate, is a statement about these classes.

LiveThree numbers from one groupby3 min▶

The number of classes. On age, sex, race, marital status, education and country of birth, the 32,561 rows fall into 8,553 classes.

The number of rows in a class of one. 5,594 rows, 17.18% of the file, share their six values with nobody. Each is a person an outsider who knows those six facts can single out.

The smallest class size. That is k, the subject of Engineer session 2. Here k is 1, and no release with k of 1 is anonymised in any useful sense.

Self-studyIdentity, attribute and inference disclosure3 min▶

PDPC's Guide to Basic Anonymisation (Step 5) names three kinds of disclosure a release must manage: identity disclosure, where a record is tied to a person; attribute disclosure, where something about a person is learned; and inference disclosure, where something is deduced from the released data. A class of one invites identity disclosure, and the linkage attack in Engineer session 4 measures it. Reading a value that every member of a class shares is attribute disclosure, Engineer session 3. Inference from patterns in the data is the gap differential privacy addresses, Engineer session 5. Keep the three apart: a release can pass one test and fail the other two.

Part 3 · covers the uniqueness climb

Each ordinary column multiplies the people who are alone

5 min live

Add the quasi-identifiers one at a time and count the rows in a class of one. The curve is flat for the first two columns and then climbs steeply, because each new column splits every existing class. It is the most useful single chart in a release review: it shows which column is doing the identifying.

LiveWhy the curve bends upward3 min▶

Columns multiply. Age has 73 values, sex 2, race 5, marital status 7, education 16, country of birth 42. The possible combinations multiply to 3,433,920, against 32,561 people. Most combinations are empty, and the people in the rare ones are alone.

Skew makes it worse. Most rows are white, US-born and in a handful of education levels. The few who are not land in tiny classes. Uniqueness falls hardest on minorities, migrants and people with rare jobs or degrees, which is exactly who a careless release exposes first.

Size helps less than you hope. A bigger file puts more people in each class, but the curve keeps its shape. A national dataset with date of birth and postcode is far more unique than this one.

Self-studyWhat the research found on whole populations3 min▶

Sweeney estimated from 1990 US Census summaries that ZIP code, full date of birth and sex were likely to single out about 87% of the US population. Golle re-ran the estimate on the 2000 Census and found about 63%, still a majority. Rocher, Hendrickx and de Montjoye (2019) fitted a model that estimated 99.98% of Americans could be correctly re-identified in any dataset using 15 demographic attributes. The numbers differ because the columns, years and methods differ; the direction does not. Your own file's climb, measured as above, is the number your release review needs.

Real world

Searches with no names. In 2006 AOL released about 20 million web search queries with each user replaced by a number. Reporters from The New York Times read the queries of user 4417749 and traced them to Thelma Arnold, a 62-year-old widow in Lilburn, Georgia. Search terms were never thought of as quasi-identifiers. Any column about a person can become one.

Build-along

Count who is alone in Brightwater's extract

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Dr Hana Seow, Brightwater's research director, has three university partners asking for the extract. Ravi Menon, the data engineer, has already removed names and IDs and wants to send it. Before he does, measure what the file still reveals. Use the widget below in the browser, or run the pandas code on your own machine against the same public file; both print the same numbers.

Step 1 · List every column. Write down every column in the extract, including the ones you think are harmless. Brightwater's file has fifteen; the list below is the real UCI Adult header.

★ Step 1 · the columnsage, workclass, fnlwgt, education, education_num, marital, occupation, relationship, race, sex, capital_gain, capital_loss, hours, country, income

Step 2 · Sort them into three bins. Direct identifier, quasi-identifier or sensitive. A column that is both, mark both. Note that education_num is a copy of education and relationship largely repeats marital status and sex: a duplicate column is still a quasi-identifier.

★ Step 2 · three binsdirect identifiers: (none in this extract; names and IDs already removed) quasi-identifiers: age, sex, race, marital, education, country, workclass, relationship, education_num, hours ______________ sensitive: occupation, income, capital_gain, capital_loss ______ not sure yet: fnlwgt (a census weight) ___________________________

Step 3 · Write what an outsider could know. Name a realistic outsider: a colleague, a neighbour, someone with the person's social media profile. List the columns they could fill in. Brightwater's partners are university researchers; their students are an outsider too.

★ Step 3 · the outsideroutsider: a colleague of someone in the file they know: age, sex, race, marital status, education, country of birth they might: workclass (public sector or private), hours worked they want: occupation detail, income band

Step 4 · Measure the climb. Tick the columns in the widget one at a time, in the order you listed them, and record the unique share each time. Or download the public file (archive.ics.uci.edu, dataset 2, adult.zip), unzip it next to this script and run it.

★ Step 4 · pandas, the same numbersimport pandas as pd cols = ["age", "workclass", "fnlwgt", "education", "education_num", "marital", "occupation", "relationship", "race", "sex", "capital_gain", "capital_loss", "hours", "country", "income"] df = pd.read_csv("adult.data", names=cols, skipinitialspace=True) qi = ["age", "sex", "race", "marital", "education", "country"] for i in range(1, len(qi) + 1): size = df.groupby(qi[:i])["age"].transform("size") print(f"+ {qi[i - 1]:<10} unique: {100 * (size == 1).mean():.2f}%") # prints 0.01% 0.02% 0.20% 1.73% 10.39% 17.18%

Step 5 · Write the finding in one sentence. The finding has three parts: the share alone, the columns that did it, and the outsider who could use it. This sentence goes at the top of the release request, and Engineer session 2 starts from it.

★ Step 5 · the findingOn ______ columns an outsider could know, ____% of rows are in a class of one; the column that does most of the identifying is ________. Brightwater: on six columns a colleague could know, 17.18% of 32,561 rows are alone; education takes it from 1.73% to 10.39%.

Keep the inventory. The three-bin sort and the climb are inputs to every later session. If a column moves bins later because you learn an outsider could know it, re-run the climb; the release decision changes with it.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Engineer session 1 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Brightwater's extract has no names or IDs. On age, sex, race, marital status, education and country of birth, what share of its 32,561 real rows are in a class of one?

Removing direct identifiers leaves the quasi-identifiers, and six ordinary facts put more than one in six people alone.

2 · What makes a column a quasi-identifier?

Age is not unique and not called an ID, yet with five other ordinary columns it singles people out. What an outsider can know decides the bin.

3 · Why does adding education to age, sex, race and marital status raise the unique share from 1.73% to 10.39%?

Combinations multiply while the number of people stays the same, so the rare combinations empty out into classes of one.
